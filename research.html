Research

From molecular mechanisms to material performance.

I investigate how polymer architecture, thermodynamic interactions and processing history determine the structure, mechanics and degradation of soft and polymeric materials.

A connected research programme

Structure is not just a descriptor. It is a mechanism.

In polymeric materials, the connectivity and organization of chains span multiple length scales. These features influence sorption, crystallization, mechanical response, interfacial integrity and the pathways by which materials age or fragment. My work combines molecular-level models with thermodynamics and multiphysics simulation to make these connections explicit.

Across these themes, I seek mechanistic links between molecular architecture, mesoscale morphology and macroscopic behaviour—and ways to use those links to design more reliable and sustainable materials.

01

Semicrystalline polymers: topology, morphology and interfaces

Semicrystalline polymers combine ordered crystallites with amorphous chain segments. I study how chain connectivity, tie molecules and chain ends shape the topology of the amorphous phase, and how the history of melting, interdiffusion and recrystallization affects interfacial structure. Recent work has examined melt-memory effects during thermal welding and the persistence of structural inhomogeneities near weld planes.

02

Polymer degradation and micro-/nanoplastics

I investigate the role of polymer microstructure—particularly amorphous chains and the organization of semicrystalline domains—in the formation and release of micro- and nanoplastics. This work connects degradation mechanisms and photooxidation to changes in microstructure and mechanics, with a broader interest in predictive descriptions of polymer lifetime and material performance.

NanoplasticsPhotooxidationAmorphous domainsPolymer aging
03

Thermodynamics and fluid sorption in polymers

My doctoral research developed molecular thermodynamic descriptions of fluid sorption in semicrystalline and amorphous polymers. This includes SAFT-γ Mie group-contribution modelling of water–polyethylene glycol mixtures, hydrocarbon sorption in polyethylene and CO₂ solubility in polystyrene and PMMA, combined with statistical-mechanical descriptions of semicrystalline polymer morphology and non-equilibrium glassy-polymer behaviour.

SAFT-γ MieFluid sorptionEquations of stateStatistical thermodynamics
04

Processing, mechanics and multiphysics interfaces

I contribute to modelling frameworks for thermoplastic processing in which thermal transport, crystallization, deformation and nanoscale interdiffusion are coupled. A central application is the formation and performance of interfaces in overmolded thermoplastic parts. Related work addresses thermomechanically coupled visco–hyperelastic–plastic behaviour and self-heating in thermoplastics.

OvermoldingChemo-thermo-mechanicsInterfacial integrityConstitutive modelling
05

Polymer networks, gels and programmable matter

My current work also addresses the thermodynamics and phase behaviour of polymer networks, including reversible crosslinking and networks under tension. Other research activities include gel thermodynamics and multiphysics simulation of DNA self-assembly, connecting soft-matter physics to bio-programmable materials.

Polymer networksGel thermodynamicsReversible crosslinksDNA self-assembly
06

Methods: theory, computation and experiment

The common approach is to combine physical modelling with computation and experimental characterization. My technical experience includes statistical mechanics, polymer physics and condensed matter; molecular dynamics, Monte Carlo and density functional theory; scientific programming and HPC; and SAXS/WAXS, calorimetry, thermogravimetric analysis, GC–MS, FTIR and Raman spectroscopy. Closed-loop AI-driven experimentation is an emerging direction in my research activities.
